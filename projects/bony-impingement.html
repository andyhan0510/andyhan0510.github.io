Extra-Articular Bony Impingement in Total Hip Arthroplasty

May 2021 – Dec 2023 · Ph.D. dissertation, Chapter 3 · Published in Journal of Orthopaedic Research (2026)

Background & approach

Extra-articular bony impingement may contribute to posture-specific groin pain and instability after THA, but the relative roles of bone shape and functional pelvic tilt were unclear.

CT reconstructions of 100 cadavers (200 hips) were used to build 3D bone-on-bone collision models. Femora were rotated internally/externally and in abduction/adduction from 30° hyperextension to 120° flexion, with pelvic tilt varied ±30° from neutral.

Key findings

  • Mean external rotation to impingement in extension was 46.6° ± 14.8°; mean internal rotation at 90° flexion was 34.4° ± 14.1°.
  • 30° posterior pelvic tilt reduced standing external-rotation clearance by 13.2°; 30° anterior tilt reduced seated internal-rotation clearance by 33.0° (p < 0.001).
  • Standing impingement mostly involved the ischial tuberosity; seated impingement mostly involved the anterior inferior iliac spine (AIIS).
  • Multivariable models with nested cross-validation identified high-risk bony phenotypes that impinged markedly earlier under adverse pelvic tilt.

Significance

The study quantifies posture-dependent bony constraints and identifies morphology-based risk groups that could be screened for before surgery.

Methods & tools

MATLAB collision detection, CT-based 3D bone models, LASSO, multivariable regression, nested cross-validation
