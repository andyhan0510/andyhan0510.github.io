Native Iliopsoas–Bone Contact and Excursion Across Hip Postures

Dec 2023 – Nov 2026 · Ph.D. dissertation, Chapter 4 · Accepted for presentation at ORS 2027

Background & approach

Internal snapping hip and iliopsoas impingement are common hip pathologies, yet the mechanics of iliopsoas contact with the pelvis and femur had not been characterized at population scale.

I developed a semi-automated, CT-based finite element workflow in which a deformable iliopsoas with spatially varying material properties wraps over the iliopubic eminence and inserts on the lesser trochanter. Each of 200 hips was extended from 30° flexion to full extension and then rotated 30° internally or externally.

Key findings

  • Pelvic contact localized to the iliopubic eminence, increasing in external rotation and decreasing in internal rotation.
  • Femoral-head contact concentrated in the anterior 2–4 o’clock region; femoral-neck contact occurred in 38 hips (19%) at 30° internal rotation.
  • Iliopubic excursion was greater in extension (9.05 mm) than in internal or external rotation (2.91 and 3.19 mm).
  • Among 28 CT-derived measurements, lesser-trochanter position and femoral inclination were the main correlates of iliopubic excursion.

Significance

These results provide a native baseline for studying snapping hip, iliopsoas impingement, and its treatment, and served as the reference model for evaluating the iliopsoas after virtual hip replacement.

Methods & tools

Abaqus/Explicit, Simpleware ScanIP, HyperMesh, MATLAB, Python, correlation analysis with FDR control
