Automated Acetabular Cup Planning and Native Anatomy Restoration (Auto-Cup)

Jan 2026 – Nov 2026 · Ph.D. dissertation, Chapter 5 · Accepted for presentation at ORS 2027

Background & approach

Surgeons commonly aim to restore the patient’s native hip center during THA, but cup placement is limited by bony constraints, cup coverage, exposed cup surface, and the amount of bone removed. A patient’s acetabulum therefore permits a range of feasible positions rather than a single target.

I developed a CT-based framework that generates all candidate cup positions and keeps those with >90% of the upper cup surface within the acetabular rim and >70% of the fixation surface in contact with bone. The best-fit position is the valid one closest to the native center. The framework was verified against 24 cadaveric THAs with postoperative optical scans and then applied to 200 native hips.

Key findings

  • Best-fit placement was a median 2.60 mm from the experimentally implanted cup (native-center 3.40 mm, teardrop 5.85 mm); the closest valid position was within RMS 1.43 ± 0.17 mm.
  • Best-fit and surgically implanted placements did not differ in coverage or bone removal.
  • In 200 hips, the native acetabular center was a valid cup position in 183 hips (91.5%).
  • Teardrop referencing gave the greatest coverage (95.6%) and least anterior exposure but required the most bone removal (16.0 cm³).
  • Rim and fossa-floor geometry were associated with coverage, bone removal, and anterior prominence; native version and depth mainly with prominence.

Significance

Patient-specific cup-center maps can help surgeons balance hip-center restoration, host-bone coverage, bone preservation, and anterior containment before final cup placement.

Methods & tools

MATLAB, CT segmentation and 3D reconstruction, optical scanning, registration, constrained optimization
