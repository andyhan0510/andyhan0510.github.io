Iliopsoas Contact and Excursion After Virtual Total Hip Arthroplasty

May 2026 – Nov 2026 · Ph.D. dissertation, Chapter 6 · Manuscript in preparation

Background & approach

Iliopsoas irritation is a common cause of persistent groin pain after total hip arthroplasty (THA), but the mechanics of psoas impingement on the implant remain incompletely characterized.

This project combined three models developed in my doctoral work: the CT-based native iliopsoas finite element model, the anatomy-constrained acetabular cup-placement framework, and an experimentally validated femoral implantation algorithm. Together they produced 130 virtually reconstructed hips from 65 specimens, which were simulated through passive extension and gait.

Key findings

  • Psoas–cup contact occurred in 26.9% of hips during extension and 36.2% during gait, compared with 44.6% and 73.8% for psoas–femoral head contact.
  • Among hips with contact, gait contact excursion was greater on the cup than on the femoral head (8.7 ± 5.5 vs. 3.4 ± 1.8 mm).
  • Maximum anterior cup overhang correlated with the likelihood of psoas–cup contact (Spearman’s ρ = 0.47–0.50).
  • During gait, cup contact occurred in 61.3% of hips with both anteroposterior and mediolateral overhang, vs. 14.3% and 16.7% with isolated overhang, and in none of the 11 hips without overhang.
  • In 47 hips with contact, high-offset femoral stems reduced cumulative cup contact area (mean difference −3.5 mm²; Holm-adjusted p = 0.006).

Significance

The results indicate that psoas–cup contact is most likely when the cup overhangs in both directions, and that femoral stem offset is a modifiable factor that may reduce this contact. Both components should be evaluated together when planning THA.

Methods & tools

Abaqus/Explicit, MATLAB, CT-based finite element modeling, virtual implantation, Spearman correlation, Holm-adjusted paired comparisons
