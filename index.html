About Me

I received my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering (November 2026) from the Center for Orthopaedic Biomechanics, University of Denver, where I worked with Dr. Chadd Clary. My research develops patient-specific computational models of the hip, built from CT imaging, finite element analysis, and statistical methods, to understand why some patients develop complications after total hip arthroplasty (THA) and how surgical planning can prevent them.

THA is one of the most common joint-replacement procedures in the United States, yet impingement, instability, and iliopsoas tendon irritation remain frequent causes of pain, revision surgery, and healthcare cost. My dissertation, “Influence of Patients’ Hip Anatomy on Common Complications after Total Hip Arthroplasty,” combines population-scale virtual surgery in hundreds of CT-derived hips with cadaveric validation to identify which anatomical features put patients at risk, and to give surgeons quantitative, patient-specific guidance for implant selection and placement.

Before my doctoral work, I earned an M.S. in Bioengineering at the University of Denver, where I worked on experimental and computational evaluation of patellofemoral implants in knee replacement.

Current Research

Iliopsoas impingement after hip replacement

Groin pain from the iliopsoas tendon rubbing on the acetabular cup is a common, under-diagnosed complication of THA. Using CT-based finite element models of 200 native hips and 130 virtually reconstructed hips, I quantify where and how much the tendon contacts bone and implants during extension and gait, showing that cup overhang drives contact (61.3% of hips with combined overhang) and that high-offset femoral stems significantly reduce it.

Automated acetabular cup planning (Auto-Cup)

A CT-based framework that maps every anatomically admissible cup position for a given patient and selects cup size and pose under rim-containment and bone-contact constraints. Verified against 24 cadaveric hips (median 2.60 mm from the surgically implanted cup) and applied to 200 hips to quantify trade-offs between restoring the hip center, bone coverage, and bone removal.

Pelvic tilt and bony impingement

A 3D collision-detection framework in 200 hips showing that patient posture matters: 30° posterior pelvic tilt reduced standing external-rotation clearance by 13.2°, and 30° anterior tilt reduced seated internal-rotation clearance by 33.0°. Machine-learning and regression analyses identify anatomical risk phenotypes that surgeons can screen for pre-operatively.
